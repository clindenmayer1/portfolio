The challenge

How do you completely redesign a product people already depend on?

The objective

A modern B2B storefront that makes complex purchasing simple, without disrupting what already works.

Teledynamics had an established customer base of professional buyers who rely on the platform to purchase office supplies, networking equipment, electronics, and more for businesses of all sizes. The experience needed a major overhaul, but starting over wasn't an option. We had to modernize it without disrupting the workflows customers already depended on.

View live site ↗

From prototype to system

I started by building the experience as a working prototype, using AI-assisted tools to move quickly through iterations. That let me test the product in context and see which patterns were actually worth standardizing. Those patterns started to define the foundations of the design system.

Those foundations became a token architecture in Figma, organized across primitive, semantic, and component layers. I used Style Dictionary to transform the tokens into CSS and TypeScript, creating a direct connection between the design system and the component library. I then built and tested the components in Storybook, giving developers one place to find, review, and implement them. From there, I focused on how the system would be documented and governed so it could grow without losing consistency.

System governance

I built a dedicated Design Center to make the system easier to understand and use. It brings together the foundations, components, implementation guidance, and governance in one place so the system can scale without losing consistency.

View the Design Center ↗

The outcome

A product like Envision would have once required a much larger team, budget, and timeline to even prove out. New tools changed that equation. I was able to take the idea from concept to working product, then build the design system and infrastructure around it. More importantly, it let me push product thinking much further before engineering investment was required, so decisions could be tested, refined, and proven with far less risk.
